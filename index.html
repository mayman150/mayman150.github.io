About

I am a reinforcement learning researcher at Amazon, based in Canada. My work sits at the seam between learning theory and systems that have to keep working after the distribution moves.

The through-line across everything below is the same stubborn problem. A long trajectory produces one noisy outcome, and something has to decide which of the thousand intermediate decisions earned it. In language-model reasoning that shows up as segment-level credit assignment. In continual learning it shows up as a step size that has to tell drift apart from gradient noise. In multi-agent settings it shows up as an agent trying to value a peer's behaviour without ever observing that peer's reward.

Before this I spent a long time close to the metal — pipelined RISC-V in Verilog, an x86-64 bootloader that walks itself into long mode, a distributed system in Rust with its own election protocol. That background still shapes how I do research: I would rather measure the quantity everyone assumes than assume it.

Currently

Running variance measurements on reasoning traces, and proving transient bounds for a shift-aware step-size rule. Open to conversations about either.
